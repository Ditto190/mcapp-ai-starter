# MEMENTO.md — Recent Working Context and Lessons Learned

## What we worked on recently
We have been using this workspace primarily to improve a Modern Airline Retailing (MAR) Statement of Work and the supporting agent operating model around it.

Recent tasks included:
- creating an initial AGENTS.md for the SOW generation environment
- reviewing the current SOW draft in detail
- identifying structural and commercial issues in the draft
- clarifying what PowerPoint solutioning templates are available in the workspace
- defining change-management terminology in SOW-ready language
- repairing Business Enablement deliverables and improving milestone-based delivery logic

## Main topics covered
- SOW orchestration and agent onboarding
- contract-grade SOW review
- proposal and solutioning reference material
- change-management definitions
- Business Enablement deliverables
- milestone-based deliverable design
- traceability between scope, deliverables, acceptance, and pricing

## Pain points observed
1. The current draft has strong content but weak contract structure.
2. Scope, responsibilities, dependencies, and assumptions have been blending together.
3. Several deliverables were conceptually right but too vague to be acceptance-ready.
4. Business Enablement deliverables needed clearer cadence across milestones.
5. The user often wanted the exact artifact fixed, not a broad conceptual lecture.
6. Generic “what next” suggestions were less helpful than precise rewrites.

## Important findings from the draft review
- The biggest draft weakness is broken traceability:
  scope -> deliverables -> acceptance -> pricing
- Responsibilities were embedded inside in-scope services.
- Dependencies were sometimes written as program assumptions rather than tangible client inputs.
- Deliverables such as Operating Model, Change Tracking, and Performance Support Materials were underdefined.
- Acceptance criteria were too generic to protect against dispute.
- Pricing language was not yet tightly linked to deliverable coverage or capacity logic.

## What worked well in the interaction
- Starting with a structural diagnosis before editing wording.
- Reframing vague items into contract-grade deliverables with testable descriptions.
- Distinguishing foundational, progressive, and per-milestone deliverables.
- Using direct, client-facing, SOW-ready definitions instead of theory.
- Bringing change-management content back to service language rather than outcome language.

## Lessons to retain
### For SOW work
- Fix structure before polishing prose.
- Separate every statement into exactly one section category.
- Never leave a deliverable unnamed, undefined, or unmapped.
- Use placeholders rather than inventing dates, rates, or facts.
- Treat the current draft as a live artifact, not as a correct source of rules.

### For Business Enablement work
- Use three cadence types when helpful:
  - foundational deliverables
  - progressive deliverables
  - per-milestone operational deliverables
- Distinguish training from performance support.
- Distinguish change strategy from change tracking.
- Keep change-management language about enabling transition, not guaranteeing adoption.

### For user interaction
- Be direct and practical.
- Go straight to the requested artifact or definition.
- Avoid overloading the response with optional follow-up paths unless invited.
- Make improvements usable in the table, not just analytically correct.
